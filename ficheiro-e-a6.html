What Is the Old Testament?

A simple and direct explanation designed for any adult — even those without theological knowledge.

Photo by Taylor Flowe on Unsplash

1. A Collection of Ancient Books

The Old Testament is the first part of the Bible. It is not a single book, but a collection of writings composed over many centuries before the birth of Jesus. It includes history, laws, poetry, prophecies, and moral teachings.

2. Main Contents

  • The story of creation and human origins.
  • The history of the people of Israel — patriarchs, the Exodus, conquest, monarchy, exile, and restoration.
  • Laws and instructions — regulating religious, social, and moral life.
  • Poetry and wisdom — psalms, proverbs, and reflections on life.
  • Prophecies — messages of judgment, calls to repentance, and future promises.

3. Important Figures

The Old Testament presents figures such as Abraham, Moses, David, Solomon, Isaiah, and other prophets who played central roles in the religious and moral history of the people.

4. Main Functions

  • Explain origins — where we come from and why we exist.
  • Form identity — it tells the story and traditions of the chosen people.
  • Provide moral guidance — it gives laws and ethical principles.
  • Point to promises — it announces hope and a restoring future (for many, the Messiah).

5. Relationship to the New Testament

The Old Testament prepares the ground for the New Testament. Many promises, symbols, and prophecies in the Old Testament are understood by Christians as fulfilled in Jesus. To understand Christianity, knowing the Old Testament is essential.

6. Relevance Today

Despite its antiquity, the Old Testament remains relevant. It offers wisdom, historical understanding, ethical values, and a vision of the relationship between God and humanity.

Conclusion

The Old Testament is the historical and spiritual foundation that explains the origin, identity, and hope of the biblical people. It is essential for understanding the full narrative of the Bible and how religious traditions interpret the past and the future.
