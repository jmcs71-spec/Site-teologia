Forgiving Others According to the Holy Bible

In the Bible, forgiving others means choosing to release the person who offended us from the moral debt we feel they owe us. It does not mean ignoring the wrongdoing, but choosing not to hold resentment or seek revenge.

Simply put: forgiving means giving up the right to repay evil for evil.

1. God Commands Forgiveness

Forgiveness is presented as a command for all who follow God.

"Forgive one another, just as God forgave you in Christ." (Ephesians 4:32)

2. We Forgive Because We Have Been Forgiven

The model for human forgiveness is God's own forgiveness.

"Just as Christ forgave you, so you also must do." (Colossians 3:13)

3. Forgiveness Has No Fixed Limits

We should forgive repeatedly.

"I do not say to you up to seven times, but up to seventy times seven." (Matthew 18:22)

4. Forgiving Does Not Mean Approving the Wrong

Forgiving does not mean saying that the wrongdoing was right, but not keeping evil in the heart.

"Do not be overcome by evil, but overcome evil with good." (Romans 12:21)

5. Forgiveness Brings Freedom

Holding resentment harms the offended person. The Bible encourages us to abandon bitterness.

"Let all bitterness… be put away from you." (Ephesians 4:31)

6. Forgiveness and Our Relationship with God

Forgiving others is connected to our relationship with God.

"If you forgive others their trespasses, your heavenly Father will also forgive you." (Matthew 6:14)

Conclusion

According to the Bible, forgiving others is a command, is based on God's forgiveness, and should be continuous. It does not mean approving wrongdoing, but it brings inner freedom and is connected to our relationship with God.

Forgiveness is essential for living in peace, reflecting the character of God, and maintaining healthy relationships.
